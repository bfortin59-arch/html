    <h2>The Fort’s Defensive Backbone</h2>
    <p>
      The South Wall formed one of the primary defensive lines of Fort Duffield. 
      Soldiers shaped this wall by digging a ditch and piling the earth upward to create a parapet. 
      The steep slope below made this an ideal defensive position, forcing any attacker to climb uphill under fire.
    </p>

    <p>
      The wall’s height and thickness were calculated to stop musket fire and provide cover for defenders. 
      Soldiers could fire over the top while remaining protected from return fire. 
      The ditch in front added another obstacle, slowing attackers and exposing them to defenders above.
    </p>

    <h2>Engineering on the Ridge</h2>
    <!-- Insert photo of ditch and parapet here -->

    <p>
      Civil War engineers understood the value of terrain. 
      Here, the natural slope of the ridge worked in the Union’s favor. 
      By adding an earthen wall and ditch, they transformed the hillside into a formidable barrier.
    </p>

    <p>
      Earthworks like these were faster to build than stone or timber fortifications and could absorb artillery fire more effectively. 
      Even today, the wall retains much of its original shape — a testament to the soldiers’ labor and the durability of earthen defenses.
    </p>

    <h2>Life Along the Wall</h2>
    <p>
      Soldiers stationed here spent long hours on guard duty, watching the valley below for signs of movement. 
      Although no major attack ever reached Fort Duffield, the men remained vigilant. 
      The South Wall was a quiet but essential part of the fort’s daily routine.
    </p>
