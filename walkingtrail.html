Explore Fort Duffield at Your Own Pace

This self-guided walking tour takes you through the preserved earthworks, the soldiers’ cemetery, scenic overlooks, and key historical points. Follow the numbered stations to experience the fort as soldiers did in 1861–62.

Tour Overview

  • Distance: ~1 mile
  • Difficulty: Moderate incline
  • Terrain: Natural, uneven ground
  • Time: 30–45 minutes

Station 1 – Trailhead & Orientation

Welcome to Fort Duffield. This trail follows the same route soldiers used to climb the ridge in 1861. Imagine hauling tools, timber, and supplies up this steep hillside in winter conditions.

Station 2 – Supply Road & Wagon Path

This section of the trail follows the original wagon path used to move lumber, food, and equipment to the fort. The steep grade made supply work exhausting and dangerous.

Station 3 – First Overlook: Salt River Valley

From here, soldiers monitored the Salt River crossing and the approach to West Point. This view helped protect the Union supply line from Confederate movement.

Station 4 – Earthwork Entrance

You are now entering the main defensive perimeter. These earthworks were built by hand using shovels, picks, and improvised tools. The angled walls helped deflect enemy fire.

Station 5 – Main Fort Interior

This open area once held tents, supply shelters, and workspaces. Soldiers lived, slept, and worked here while constructing the fort under harsh winter conditions.

Station 6 – Rifle Pits & Firing Positions

These shallow depressions were firing positions for infantry. Soldiers could defend the ridge while remaining partially protected by the earthworks.

Station 7 – High Overlook: Ohio River Corridor

This vantage point was critical. From here, Union forces could observe river traffic and detect Confederate movement toward Louisville.

Station 8 – Timber Cutting Area

Much of the ridge was heavily wooded in 1861. Soldiers cut trees for walls, cabins, and firewood. Clearing the ridge also improved visibility for defense.

Station 9 – Winter Camp Area

Soldiers built makeshift shelters here using logs, canvas, and whatever materials they could find. Many suffered from exposure, pneumonia, and measles.

Station 10 – Cemetery Trail Junction

This path leads to the Fort Duffield Cemetery, where many of the young soldiers who built the fort are buried. Please treat the site with respect.

Station 11 – Fort Duffield Cemetery

This quiet hillside cemetery holds the remains of soldiers who died from illness and exposure during construction. Many were teenagers or in their early twenties.

Station 12 – Return Path & Final Viewpoint

As you descend the ridge, take in the final view of West Point and the rivers. This landscape shaped the fort’s purpose and the soldiers’ daily lives.
