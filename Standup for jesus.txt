    <h2>A Hymn Born in Turbulent Times</h2>
    <p>
      “Stand Up for Jesus” was written in 1858, just three years before the Civil War erupted.  
      Its message of courage, steadfastness, and moral conviction resonated deeply with soldiers on both sides of the conflict.  
      For Union troops stationed at Fort Duffield, the hymn offered comfort during hardship and a reminder of home.
    </p>

    <h2>Why Soldiers Sang It</h2>
    <!-- Insert historic image of Civil War camp worship here -->

    <p>
      Camp worship was a central part of Civil War soldier life.  
      Chaplains, visiting ministers, or even fellow soldiers led services that included prayer, scripture reading, and hymn singing.  
      “Stand Up for Jesus” became one of the most popular hymns of the era because its themes matched the soldiers’ daily reality:
    </p>

    <ul>
      <li>Standing firm in the face of danger</li>
      <li>Persevering through hardship</li>
      <li>Finding strength beyond oneself</li>
      <li>Facing fear with courage</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Connection to Fort Duffield</h2>
    <p>
      While no specific record ties the hymn directly to a documented service at Fort Duffield,  
      the men stationed here experienced the same conditions as soldiers across the Union Army —  
      cold nights, sickness, exhaustion, and uncertainty.  
      Hymns like this one were sung around campfires, in makeshift shelters, and during moments of reflection.
    </p>

    <h2>A Song of Hope</h2>
    <p>
      For many soldiers, music was a lifeline.  
      It reminded them of family, church, and the world they hoped to return to.  
      “Stand Up for Jesus” became a symbol of perseverance — a message that carried them through the winter of 1861–62.
    </p>
