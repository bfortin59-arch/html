A Quiet Resting Place on the Ridge

Just beyond the fort’s earthworks lies a small hillside cemetery where many of the young soldiers who built Fort Duffield were laid to rest. Their graves tell a solemn story of hardship, illness, and sacrifice during the winter of 1861–62.

Who Is Buried Here

Most of the men buried in the Fort Duffield Cemetery died not from battle, but from disease and exposure while constructing the fort.

  • Pneumonia
  • Measles
  • Complications from exposure
  • Infections that spread rapidly through camp

Many were teenagers or in their early twenties. Some names are known; others remain unrecorded.

A Place of Honor

The cemetery is simple and quiet — a small clearing surrounded by trees, marked by stones and memorials placed over many decades. Volunteers maintain the site with care and respect, ensuring that the soldiers’ memory endures.

Why This Cemetery Matters

  • It preserves the human cost of building the fort
  • It connects visitors directly to the soldiers’ experience
  • It stands as a reminder of the hardships of early-war service
  • It is one of the few Civil War burial sites directly tied to fort construction

Visiting the Cemetery

A short trail leads from the main fort to the cemetery.

  • Stay on the marked path
  • Treat the site with respect
  • Avoid touching or leaning on headstones
  • Keep noise to a minimum

This is hallowed ground.
