Welcome to Fort Duffield

Fort Duffield stands as one of Kentucky’s most significant Civil War earthworks. Built in the fall of 1861 by the 9th Michigan and 37th Indiana Infantry, the fort protected the vital Union supply base at West Point during the early months of the war.

Today, the site is preserved by dedicated volunteers who maintain the fort, cemetery, trails, and interpretive features so visitors can explore this historic ridge much as soldiers did during the winter of 1861–62.

Why Fort Duffield Matters

  • It is one of the best-preserved earthen forts in Kentucky.
  • It guarded the strategic junction of the Ohio and Salt Rivers.
  • It reflects the hardships faced by Union soldiers during a brutal winter.
  • It honors the memory of those who served, including the young and the fallen.

Preservation and Volunteer Work

Fort Duffield is maintained entirely by volunteers. Their work includes trail clearing, erosion control, brush removal, cemetery care, and interpretive improvements. New volunteers are always welcome.
