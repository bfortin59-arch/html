    <h2>About the Trail</h2>
    <p>
      The walking tour follows the original military road and the interior of the fort. 
      The terrain is steep in places — just as it was for the soldiers who built it. 
      The trail is approximately one mile and includes eight interpretive stations.
    </p>

    <h2>Visitor Guidance</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Trail Difficulty:</strong> Moderate. Steep sections near the Gate and Overlook.</li>
      <li><strong>Footing:</strong> Uneven ground, exposed roots, and seasonal mud.</li>
      <li><strong>Stay on the trail:</strong> The earthworks are fragile and irreplaceable.</li>
      <li><strong>Water:</strong> Bring your own; no potable water on site.</li>
      <li><strong>Weather:</strong> The ridge is exposed — conditions can change quickly.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Introduction to the Fort</h2>
    <p>
      Fort Duffield was constructed in the fall and winter of 1861–62 by Union troops stationed at West Point, Kentucky. 
      Its purpose was to protect the vital supply line running from the Ohio River to Elizabethtown and beyond. 
      The fort’s position on the ridge offered a commanding view of the confluence of the Salt and Ohio Rivers, 
      making it a strategic point of defense.
    </p>

    <p>
      The soldiers who built the fort endured harsh weather, disease, and the physical strain of cutting roads, 
      digging earthworks, and hauling timber up the steep hillside. 
      Their work remains visible today in the preserved walls, ditches, and terraces of the fort.
    </p>
