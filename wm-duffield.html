Wm W. Duffield

Colonel William W. Duffield, for whom Fort Duffield is named, served as commander of the 9th Michigan Infantry during the early months of the Civil War. Although he never directly commanded the fort that bears his name, his leadership and service were highly respected by the men who built and occupied the ridge above West Point.

Early Life and Career

Born in Michigan, Duffield came from a family with a strong tradition of public service. Before the war, he worked as a surveyor and engineer—skills that later proved valuable in military service.

Service in the Civil War

As colonel of the 9th Michigan Infantry, Duffield led his regiment during the early campaigns in Kentucky and Tennessee. He was wounded at the Battle of Mill Springs, an injury that prevented him from returning to full field command.

Despite this, his regiment continued to honor him, and the naming of Fort Duffield reflected the respect and admiration his men held for him.

Legacy

Colonel Duffield’s name lives on through the fort, the cemetery, and the ongoing preservation efforts at West Point. His story represents the dedication and sacrifice of the Union officers who served during the early, uncertain months of the Civil War.

Today, visitors to Fort Duffield walk the same ridge where the men of his regiment labored through the winter of 1861–62, building the fort that would carry his name into history.
