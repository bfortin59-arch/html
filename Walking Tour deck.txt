    <h2>A Place of Command</h2>
    <p>
      The Deck was an elevated platform built to give officers a clear view of the fort’s interior and the surrounding terrain. 
      From here, they could direct work details, oversee guard rotations, and monitor the defensive walls. 
      In a fort built quickly and under harsh conditions, having a central vantage point was essential.
    </p>

    <h2>Communication Hub</h2>
    <!-- Insert photo of interior fort area here -->

    <p>
      Before radios or telephones, communication relied on line-of-sight, runners, and signal flags. 
      The Deck provided a place where officers could relay orders quickly across the fort. 
      Its height made it easier to see approaching wagons, supply trains, or messengers coming up the military road.
    </p>

    <h2>Daily Life Around the Deck</h2>
    <p>
      The area around the Deck would have been busy with activity. 
      Soldiers gathered here for instructions, work assignments, and updates from officers. 
      The platform also served as a vantage point for watching the valley below — a welcome distraction during long, quiet days on the ridge.
    </p>

    <div class="callout">
      <strong>Look for:</strong> The raised ground where the platform once stood and the sightlines it offered across the fort.
